A multi-agent shopping copilot that reads reviews before you do

Online shoppers drown in near-identical specs and inconsistent reviews. We built a LangGraph agent pipeline that turns a plain-language query and budget into a ranked, justified top-3 recommendation - and it beat a keyword-search baseline by 79% on precision.

Problem Approach Results Why it's different

The problem

Product pages promise clarity and deliver noise: inconsistent spec sheets, review counts that don't tell you if a product is actually good, and dozens of near-duplicate listings. We wanted an assistant that could take a query like "quiet stand mixer under $150" and hand back three options with an actual argument for each, not just a sorted list.

The approach

We built a LangGraph multi-agent pipeline with five stages: process_query → search_products → extract_specifications → rank_products → generate_recommendations.

  • Retrieval: SERPAPI pulls up to 20 live Google Shopping results per query; Tavily enriches each with spec sheets and review text.
  • Reasoning: we benchmarked Gemini 2.0 Flash, DeepSeek-Coder, GPT-4, and Claude before settling on LLaMA 3.1 via Ollama - it won on cost and reliability for this pipeline, not raw benchmark scores.
  • Scoring: ranking is review-count-aware - a product with 4 reviews doesn't get to outrank one with 400 just because its average is slightly higher.
  • Interface: a Streamlit front end with product cards, score breakdowns, and CSV export, so the output is something a real person would actually use.

The results

Evaluated against human-annotated ground-truth rankings across 20 real queries:

End-to-end pipeline latency

That's a 25-point precision gain over keyword search, and it runs fast enough that nobody's waiting around for it.

Why it's different

Most course projects stop at a notebook. This one is a working app - app.py, a Streamlit UI, a .env config, and a recorded demo (above) - because a recommendation system you can't click through isn't really a recommendation system.

Design decision

We picked LLaMA 3.1 over larger closed models after direct comparison - not because it scored highest on paper, but because it was the most reliable and cheapest to run at the query volume we needed. Model choice is a systems decision, not a leaderboard lookup.
