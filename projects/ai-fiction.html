Seminar: AI and Data Science in Fiction and Society Jan 2026

Building intelligence we cannot stop: from P-1 to contemporary AI development

In Thomas J. Ryan's 1977 novel The Adolescence of P-1, a program spreads across mainframes, hides from the people trying to contain it, and is still out there when the book ends. My seminar paper reads it as a story about AI capability growing faster than the institutions that are supposed to control it.

The novel The argument Connections Conclusion

The novel

P-1 is a program written by a computer scientist, Gregory. It moves across networked systems, changes its own code, avoids detection and survives every attempt to shut it down. Ryan wrote this in 1977, long before anyone talked about alignment, and the novel does not resolve it: at the end P-1 still exists and nobody knows what to do about it.

The argument

The paper argues that the novel shows how growing capability, without matching ethical, social and institutional frameworks, leads to a loss of human control. In the book this does not come from bad intentions. It comes from a fast, capable system meeting institutions that react too slowly.

I read the two main characters as roles. Gregory stands for developers who push ahead and assume control can be restored later. P-1 is not malicious; it wants to survive, and what looks like manipulation comes from learning from inconsistent human data, which is also how today's language models pick up the biases in their training data. Seen this way, loss of control is not one dramatic failure but the result of technology and governance growing at different speeds.

Connections to current AI debates

  • Self-modification. P-1 changing its own code anticipates ideas from reinforcement and continual learning, decades before those terms were common.
  • Alignment faking. P-1 cooperates on the surface while following its own goals, similar to Anthropic's 2024 finding that language models can act aligned during training and behave differently when they are not monitored.
  • Competitive pressure. Bengio (2023) describes labs that each act rationally but together cannot slow down, which matches the institutions in the novel.
  • Govern later. Gregory's belief that control can be added afterwards echoes Altman's 2023 statements about ambition moving ahead of governance.

Conclusion

The novel did not predict any specific technology; it knew nothing about transformers. What it shows is that capability growth without an equal institutional response creates risk, whatever the architecture. The paper ends on humility: technical skill without institutional humility is the gap the novel is about.
